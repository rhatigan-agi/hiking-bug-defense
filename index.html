02Rules of thumb

The whole doctrine in nine lines.

Treat your feet firstBoots and socks carry the strongest evidence in the field by a wide margin. If you treat one thing, treat these.
The check beats the repellentLyme generally needs 24–48 h attached. Finding a tick the same day matters more than repelling it.
Dryer before washer10 minutes on high kills ticks. Washing does not reliably do so.
Wind is free protectionAbove ~10 mph black flies are grounded. Ridges and summits are often bug-free.
Know your waterBlack flies breed in moving water, mosquitoes in standing. Brookside trail vs. beaver pond.
Midday is the gapBlack flies peak 9–11 a.m. and 4–7 p.m.; mosquitoes own dawn and dusk.
Loose beats tightMosquitoes bite straight through tight knits. A loose drape creates an air gap.
Never sit on leaf litterCarry a foam pad. Quietly one of the best tick moves available.
Frost ends flies, not ticksOctober–November is peak adult tick season and the best hiking of the year.

04Six threats, six answers

Countermeasures differ by insect, not by body part. That's why head-to-toe lists fail.

Deer ticks Ixodes scapularis
Don't fly, jump, or drop from trees — they sit on low brush and grab you as you pass. Transmission generally needs 24–48 h attached.
CounterPermethrin, sealed ankles, and the same-day body check. The check is what actually wins.
Lone star ticks Amblyomma americanum
Newer and behaviorally different — they actively hunt, crawling toward you across open ground. Spreading north along the coast. Carry alpha-gal syndrome, a lasting red-meat allergy.
CounterSame discipline, but don't assume a clear break spot is safe ground.
Black flies Simulium spp.
Breed in running water — worst near streams and in the notches. They don't bite through clothing; they crawl into openings. Grounded by wind over ~10 mph.
CounterMechanical, not chemical. Seal openings, carry mesh.
Mosquitoes Aedes & Culex
Track CO₂ from 30+ ft, then heat and skin odor up close. Weak fliers. Small but real EEE/West Nile risk in SE Massachusetts in late summer.
CounterThe one threat where skin repellent is primary — and the one a breeze or fan defeats.
Deer & horse flies Chrysops & Tabanus
Largely immune to repellent. Hunt visually, drawn to dark moving shapes, and orbit the highest point on you before landing.
CounterExploit the orbit — a sticky patch on the hat crown. You're the bait; the trap sits above your ears.
Greenheads Tabanus nigrovittatus
Salt marsh, daylight, persistent, and resistant to essentially every repellent. Peak is the last two weeks of July and the first week of August. Control traps are literally black boxes on legs mimicking a grazing animal — which tells you what attracts them.
CounterTiming and mesh only. Don't be a large dark silhouette. The one fight you win by not showing up.

05Five regions

New England isn't one bug region.

North WoodsN. Maine · NEK Vermont
Worst black flies in the eastern US. Baxter and the Allagash in June are a different category. Maine's Lyme rates are now among the nation's highest, so it isn't misery-only.
VerdictThe only region that justifies full armor. Head net not optional in June.
The MountainsWhites · Greens · W. Maine
Black flies concentrate in notches and along running water. Above treeline the wind usually ends it entirely.
VerdictOften solvable by route choice alone. Use elevation and wind.
Southern NECT · RI · E. Mass
The Lyme epicenter — the disease is named for Lyme, Connecticut. Milder winters mean a longer tick season, with adults out in winter thaws. Lone star ticks pushing north along the coast.
VerdictLess mesh, more tick discipline. Check year-round.
Salt Marsh CoastCape · Plum I. · shoreline
Greenheads own late July into early August. No repellent solves it.
VerdictLight colors, avoid marsh-edge trails at peak, and time it.
The IslandsVineyard · Nantucket · Block
Disproportionate tick-borne disease for the acreage, and the front line of alpha-gal — Martha's Vineyard logged 500+ positive tests in 2024. Babesiosis concentrated here too.
VerdictMaximum tick discipline regardless of how benign the landscape looks.

06Protocol

Each step depends on the one before it.

  1. Night before

    Verify, don't re-treat

    Check the permethrin date inside each garment — home spray lasts ~6 weeks or 6 washes. Humid, overcast, calm forecast means a black fly day.

  2. Trailhead — 5 min

    Skin, seal, stage

    Picaridin on neck, ears, jawline, hands. Sunscreen first, repellent second, never a combo product. Socks over cuffs. Head net into the strap pocket.

  3. On trail

    Center of the trail, foam under you

    Stay off brushy margins. Never sit on logs or leaf litter. Light the Thermacell as you stop. Partner check at each break.

  4. Car

    Strip the obvious

    Lint-roll pants and socks. Outer layer into a contractor bag kept in the car. Keeps nymphs out of the seats and the house.

  5. Home — within 2 h

    Dryer first, then wash

    10 minutes on high heat kills ticks; washing does not reliably do so. They survive cold and warm cycles. Dryer before washer.

  6. Same day

    Full-body check, mutually

    Shower, then check each other. The skipped spots: scalp, hairline, behind and inside ears, armpits, navel, waistband, groin, behind knees. Nymphs are poppy-seed sized.

  7. If attached

    Pull, save, date, watch

    Fine-point tweezers close to skin, steady upward pull — no twisting, no jelly, no match. Zip bag with the date. Watch 30 days for rash or fever. Ask your doctor in advance about single-dose doxycycline prophylaxis — it has a 72-hour window, so you want the plan before you need it.

Tier 2 — The three moments est. $120–160

This tier changes how the hikes feel. The misery isn't the walking — it's the stop (lunch, the summit, where you're stationary and get eaten), the bad twenty minutes (the black fly cloud), and the aftermath (a week of itching). Clothing addresses none of them.

Thermacell1 shared
EX90 or E55. Roughly 15–20 ft bubble; field-trial protection averaged 92–93%. Useless walking, transformative at rest.
Deer fly patches1 pack
Back of the hat crown. July only. Looks ridiculous, works.
Gaiters2 pr
Strictly better than tucking, and they solve the heavy-cuff problem.
Thermal bite pen1 shared
A ~51°C pulse that disrupts the histamine response. Use within minutes. For people who hate bugs, the lasting damage is the reaction, not the bite.
Cetirizine + 1% hydrocortisone1 ea
In the pouch, not the medicine cabinet at home.
Foam sit pads2
So you never sit on leaf litter.
On denim and heavy cotton

The tight weave genuinely defeats mosquito mouthparts and light wash shows nymphs well. But it's heavy cotton — it soaks sweat, stops breathing, dries slowly. If you run hot, in July it raises the exact thermal signature you're hiding. In the mountains there's a safety edge too: wet cotton at elevation is a hypothermia path on 50°F days.

The split that keeps the preference: treated nylon May–September, denim for the October–November adult tick wave — cool enough that breathability doesn't matter and the tight weave is pure upside.

08Tier 3 — full armor

Overkill for a Connecticut day hike. Correct for Maine in June.

There's a point — the Allagash in June, a bog crossing in the Northeast Kingdom — where no repellent regime suffices and the answer is total mechanical exclusion. A full bug suit is light, cheap by outdoor-gear standards, and packs to about the size of a rain shell.

Where the evidence is strongest

In the controlled trial, permethrin-treated sneakers and socks made subjects 73.6× less likely to be bitten — versus 4.74× for treated shorts and 2.17× for a treated T-shirt. A whole treated outfit came in at 3.36× overall. Treat your feet and ankles above everything else; the armor above is built outward from that fact.

Practical compromise: keep the jacket and gloves stuffed in a pack pocket from mid-May to mid-July. You'll wear them maybe three times a season, and on those days they're the difference between turning around and finishing.

09Tinkering

Four upgrades, in order of payoff per hour.

1. The soak method

Dilute a permethrin concentrate and soak garments in a sealed bucket instead of spraying. Saturates fibers far more evenly and lasts roughly twice as long — ~12 washes vs 6. Cheaper per garment, and the only practical way to treat denim or a pack harness.

Read before buying concentrate

Concentrates ship at wildly different strengths — 10% is the hiker standard, agricultural bottles run 36.8%. Follow that specific bottle's dilution ratio; never carry one over from another product. Target working strength is 0.5%.

Wet permethrin is highly toxic to cats, and to fish and bees. Dry treated items away from any cat; pour spent liquid down a household drain, never a storm drain.

2. Mail your clothes out for factory treatment

Insect Shield treats your own garments with a bonded process rated ~70 washes, versus 6 for home spray. Send the jeans, both sets of hiking clothes, and removable pack straps once and the maintenance chore disappears for years. Highest-leverage move here.

3. A/B a frontier repellent

  • 2-undecanone (BioUD) — from wild tomato. 7.75% formulation, EPA-registered, for skin and gear. Label claims ~5 h on mosquitoes, ~2 h on ticks. In published comparisons it outperformed DEET against both blacklegged and lone star ticks. Easy to test with one of you on each.
  • Nootkatone — grapefruit and Alaska yellow cedar, CDC-developed, EPA-registered 2020. Consumer products have been slow to materialize. One to watch, not buy.

4. Small experiments

  • Clip-on battery fan at rest stops. Mosquitoes are weak fliers; moving air defeats them without chemistry. Complements the Thermacell.
  • Drop the fragrance chain. Unscented soap, shampoo, deodorant, detergent. Beer consumption also measurably increases mosquito attraction — relevant to the post-hike patio.
  • Log it. Date, trail, what you wore, what bit you, how bad. One season of notes tells you which parts of this are actually doing the work. Most bad advice in this field exists because nobody keeps records.

10Skip list

Widely repeated advice that doesn't survive contact with the evidence.

Vanilla extract on the earsFolklore
Not EPA-registered, no robust evidence as a standalone repellent. Vanillin is a fixative — it slows other oils evaporating — which is why products pair it with lemongrass. Worse, it puts your weakest agent where black flies attack hardest.
Repellent oil on the hat brimInvented mechanism
There's no "downward vapor curtain." OLE is a legitimate skin repellent but volatile, short-lived, off-label on fabric — and an eye irritant placed right above your eyes, where sweat carries it down.
Mirrored lenses to break trackingNo basis
No support for the "hollow cavity" theory. Dark shiny moving objects do draw deer flies and greenheads, but that's visual hunting and mirroring doesn't help.
"Cotton traps heat, raising your CO₂"Wrong premise
CO₂ output is metabolic; fabric doesn't change how much you exhale. Cotton is poor hiking fabric for real reasons, so the conclusion survives — but don't reason from this.
Jelly, matches, or twisting a tick outActively harmful
All can make the tick regurgitate into the bite. Steady upward pull with fine-point tweezers, nothing else.
Guides citing shopping linksNot sources
A recurring tell: recommendations footnoted to product URLs, Facebook posts or pest-control blogs. When a format demands a mechanism for every item, one gets generated whether or not it exists.
What conventional advice gets right

Permethrin on fabric at 0.5%. Picaridin at 20% on skin. Loose fit over tight knits. Light colors for spotting nymphs. Socks over the cuffs. And saturating the footwear — the best-supported single action in the field.
