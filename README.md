# New England Bug Doctrine

A layered, seasonal field manual for hiking New England without getting eaten — ticks, black flies, mosquitoes, deer flies and salt-marsh greenheads, each with a different countermeasure.

### → [**Read it: rhatigan-agi.github.io/hiking-bug-defense**](https://rhatigan-agi.github.io/hiking-bug-defense/)

A single self-contained page, served from `main` via GitHub Pages. Works offline once loaded, and the loadout checklist remembers your ticks per device.

## What this is

A **loadout checklist first, reasoning second** reference. It opens with a 12-slot
equipment grid — head, face, eyes, neck, torso, hands, legs, ankles, feet, pack,
pouch, perimeter — each with an icon, the item, and a color-coded chip for what
goes on it (permethrin / skin topical / mesh / carried kit). The slots are
tickable and remember their state on your device, so it works as a trailhead
packing check.

Everything below that is the reasoning, organized around three ideas:

1. **The misery lives in three moments** the usual advice ignores — the stop
   (lunch, the summit), the bad twenty minutes (the black fly cloud), and the
   aftermath (a week of itching).
2. **Countermeasures differ by insect, not by body part.** Head-to-toe
   organization is the common structural mistake — ticks, black flies and
   greenheads need genuinely different answers.
3. **New England is five bug regions, not one.** The North Woods, the mountains,
   southern New England, the salt marsh coast, and the islands.

It also carries a **skip list** — widely repeated advice (vanilla extract on the
ears, repellent oil on a hat brim, mirrored lenses) that doesn't survive contact
with the evidence, with the reasoning for each.

## Sources

Every load-bearing claim is cited in the page footer — peer-reviewed entomology, UNH Extension, CDC, EPA, and state mosquito-control districts. Where evidence is thin, the page says so.

## Disclaimer

Not medical advice. Product labels govern over anything written here — particularly for permethrin concentrate dilution, which varies by product and is genuinely worth getting right.
